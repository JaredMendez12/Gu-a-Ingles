1. Verbos modales

Los modales cambian el sentido del verbo principal: habilidad, permiso, obligación, consejo o probabilidad. Siempre van seguidos del verbo en forma base.

Reglas

  • Sin to y sin -s: She can swim (no can to swim, no cans).
  • Sin do en preguntas y negativas: Can you help me? I can't go.
  • Sí llevan to: have to, ought to, be able to, need to (semimodales).
ModalUsoEjemploEspañol
canHabilidad, permiso informal, posibilidadI can speak English. Can I come in?puedo, sé
couldHabilidad pasada, pedido amable, posibilidadI could swim at 5. Could you help me?podía, podría
be able toHabilidad en cualquier tiempoI'll be able to come tomorrow.podré
mayPermiso formal, posibilidadMay I use your phone? It may be true.¿puedo?, puede que
mightPosibilidad bajaI might go to the party.quizá
mustObligación personal, deducción seguraI must study tonight. You must be tired.debo, debes de
have toObligación externa (reglas)I have to wear a uniform.tengo que
mustn'tProhibiciónYou mustn't smoke here.no debes
don't have toNo es necesarioYou don't have to come.no tienes que
should / ought toConsejoYou should sleep more.deberías
had better ('d better)Consejo fuerte, advertenciaYou'd better hurry.más vale que
willFuturo, promesa, pedidoWill you open the door?¿abrirías?
wouldPedido cortés, deseo, hipótesisWould you like some tea?¿te gustaría?
shallSugerencia u ofrecimiento (en preguntas)Shall we start?¿empezamos?

mustn't ≠ don't have to

  • You mustn't use your phone in the exam. → Está prohibido.
  • You don't have to bring a laptop. → No es obligatorio, pero puedes.

Deducción: qué tan seguro estás

  • She must be at home; the lights are on. → Seguro que sí.
  • He might / may / could be sick. → Es posible.
  • That can't be Juan; he's in Europe. → Seguro que no.

Cuanto más a la derecha, más seguro estás. Para hablar del pasado usa must have, might have y can't have (sección 2).

Ejercicio 1A · Elige el modal

  1. You ___ smoke in the hospital. (mustn't / don't have to)
  2. Tomorrow is Sunday, so I ___ get up early. (mustn't / don't have to)
  3. ___ you speak French? (Can / Must)
  4. You look tired. You ___ go to bed. (should / can)
  5. Take an umbrella. It ___ rain. (might / must)
  6. ___ I borrow your charger? (May / Should)
  7. She has three jobs. She ___ be very busy. (must / can't)
  8. That ___ be true! It's impossible. (can't / mustn't)
  9. When I was 6, I ___ ride a bike. (could / can)
  10. ___ you like a glass of water? (Would / Will)

2. Modales en pasado

Modal + have + participio habla del pasado: arrepentimientos, deducciones y posibilidades que no ocurrieron.

EstructuraSignificadoEjemploEspañol
should have + participioEra lo correcto, pero no lo hicisteI should have studied more.Debí haber estudiado más.
shouldn't haveHiciste algo que no conveníaYou shouldn't have said that.No debiste decir eso.
could haveEra posible, pero no pasóWe could have won.Pudimos haber ganado.
would haveResultado imaginario (condicional 3)I would have helped you.Te habría ayudado.
must haveDeducción casi seguraShe must have forgotten.Seguro se le olvidó.
might have / may havePosibilidadHe might have missed the bus.Quizá perdió el camión.
can't have / couldn't haveImposibleYou can't have seen him; he's in Spain.No pudiste haberlo visto.

Pronunciación: should have suena /ˈʃʊdəv/ (should've). Por eso a veces se escribe mal should of: es un error.

3. Condicionales y wish

Hay 4 condicionales principales: el 0 y el 1 hablan de situaciones reales, el 2 de hipótesis y el 3 de un pasado que ya no se puede cambiar.

TipoEstructuraUsoEjemplo
0If + presente, presenteHechos: siempre pasaIf you heat ice, it melts.
1If + presente, will + baseReal o probable en el futuroIf it rains, I'll stay home.
2If + pasado, would + baseImaginario en el presente o futuroIf I had money, I would travel.
3If + had + participio, would have + participioPasado imaginario (no pasó)If I had studied, I would have passed.
MixtoIf + had + participio, would + basePasado imaginario con efecto hoyIf I had taken that job, I would be in Canada now.

Reglas

  • Nunca pongas will en la parte del if: If it rains (no If it will rain).
  • En el condicional 2, con I / he / she / it se recomienda were: If I were you, I'd accept. (para dar consejos)
  • La coma va solo si empiezas con if: If you need help, call me. / Call me if you need help.
  • unless = if not: I won't go unless you come.
  • Otras: as long as (siempre que), in case (por si), provided that (con tal de que).
Deseo sobre...EstructuraEjemploEspañol
El presentewish + pasadoI wish I had a car.Ojalá tuviera carro.
El pasadowish + had + participioI wish I had studied more.Ojalá hubiera estudiado más.
Algo que te molestawish + would + baseI wish you would stop talking.Ojalá dejaras de hablar.

If only... funciona igual que I wish..., pero con más emoción: If only I had known!

Ejercicio 3A · Completa

  1. If you mix red and white, you ___ (get) pink.
  2. If I ___ (see) Ana, I'll tell her.

  4. If we ___ (leave) now, we'll catch the bus.
  5. If she ___ (know) the answer, she would tell us.

  7. I ___ (buy) a house if I won the lottery.
  8. You won't pass ___ you study. (if / unless)
  9. If they had invited me, I ___ (go).
  10. I wish I ___ (can) speak Japanese.

4. Voz pasiva

La pasiva (be + participio) pone el foco en la acción o en quien la recibe, no en quien la hace; es muy común en textos técnicos.

Activa: The team developed the app. → Pasiva: The app was developed (by the team).

TiempoEstructuraEjemplo
Presente simpleam / is / are + participioEnglish is spoken worldwide.
Presente continuoam / is / are being + participioThe server is being updated.
Pasado simplewas / were + participioThe website was launched in 2024.
Pasado continuowas / were being + participioThe bug was being fixed.
Presente perfectohave / has been + participioYour password has been changed.
Pasado perfectohad been + participioThe files had been deleted.
Futuro (will)will be + participioThe results will be published tomorrow.
Going toam / is / are going to be + participioThe office is going to be painted.
Con modalesmodal + be + participioThe form must be signed.

Cuándo usarla

  • No sabes o no importa quién lo hizo: My phone was stolen.
  • En textos técnicos y científicos: The data is stored in the cloud. The samples were analyzed.
  • Menciona al agente con by solo si importa: The Mona Lisa was painted by Leonardo da Vinci.

Otras formas

  • Pasiva con get (informal): He got fired.
  • Impersonal: It is said that the movie is great. · She is said to be the best candidate.

Ejercicio 4A · Pasa a voz pasiva

  1. People speak Spanish in Mexico.
  2. Someone stole my bike.
  3. They are building a new hospital.
  4. The company has hired 50 engineers.
  5. They will announce the winners tomorrow.
  6. You must save the file.
  7. The teacher corrected the exams.
  8. Somebody had already paid the bill.

5. Estilo indirecto (reported speech)

Para contar lo que alguien dijo, el verbo suele retroceder un tiempo y cambian pronombres, tiempos y lugares.

Lo que dijo (directo)Cómo lo cuentas (indirecto)
Presente simple: “I work here.”She said (that) she worked there.
Presente continuo: “I'm studying.”He said he was studying.
Pasado simple: “I saw it.”She said she had seen it.
Presente perfecto: “I've finished.”He said he had finished.
will: “I'll call you.”She said she would call me.
can: “I can help.”He said he could help.
must: “I must go.”She said she had to go.
going to: “I'm going to quit.”He said he was going to quit.
DirectoIndirecto
nowthen
todaythat day
yesterdaythe day before
tomorrowthe next day
last weekthe week before
next weekthe following week
herethere
thisthat
agobefore

say o tell: say + algo; tell + persona + algo. She said she was tired. / She told me she was tired. (no she said me).

Preguntas (orden de afirmación, sin do):

  • “Where do you live?” → He asked me where I lived.
  • “Are you OK?” → She asked if I was OK.

Órdenes y pedidos (to + verbo):

  • “Close the door.” → She told me to close the door.
  • “Don't be late.” → He told us not to be late.
  • “Can you help me?” → She asked me to help her.

Otros verbos para reportar: explain, suggest, promise, warn, admit, deny, agree, refuse, offer, recommend.

Si lo dicho sigue siendo verdad, puedes no retroceder: He said he lives in Mérida (todavía vive ahí).

6. Gerundio (-ing) o infinitivo (to + verbo)

Algunos verbos van seguidos de -ing, otros de to + verbo, y unos pocos cambian de significado según cuál uses.

verbo + persona + towant, ask, tell, allow, expect, teach, remind, advise, encourage, inviteI want you to come.
-ing o to (mismo significado)start, begin, continue, like, love, hate, preferIt started raining / to rain.

7. Causativo: have / get something done

Usa have / get + objeto + participio cuando otra persona hace algo por ti, normalmente un servicio.

EstructuraEjemploEspañol
have + objeto + participioI had my hair cut.Me corté el pelo (en la estética).
get + objeto + participio (informal)I need to get my phone repaired.Necesito que me reparen el celular.
have + persona + baseI'll have the technician check it.Haré que el técnico lo revise.
get + persona + to + baseI got my brother to help me.Convencí a mi hermano de que me ayudara.

Compara

  • I cut my hair. → Me lo corté yo mismo.
  • I had my hair cut. → Alguien me lo cortó.

En español decimos «me corté el pelo» aunque lo haga otra persona; en inglés sí se distingue.

  5. He got his friend ___ (fix) the bike.

8. Phrasal verbs más útiles

Un phrasal verb es verbo + partícula (up, out, off, on...) y casi siempre significa algo distinto del verbo solo; apréndelos con su ejemplo.

Reglas

  • Separables (con objeto): Turn off the light = Turn the light off. Con pronombre, va en medio: Turn it off (no turn off it).
  • Inseparables: Look after your sister (no look your sister after).
  • Sin objeto: The plane took off. My car broke down.
