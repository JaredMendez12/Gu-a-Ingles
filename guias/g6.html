Cómo usar el cuaderno

Empieza con el examen diagnóstico, estudia lo que fallaste y regresa aquí cada semana para practicar las 4 habilidades.

  1. Haz el examen diagnóstico sin ayuda (40 minutos) y califícate con la sección «Respuestas».
  2. Revisa la tabla de resultados para saber qué documentos estudiar primero.
  3. Cada semana haz una lectura, una tarea de escritura y práctica de speaking de tu nivel.
  4. Cada semana completa una rutina de listening con audios gratuitos.
  5. Al final de cada mes, haz el repaso mixto de tu nivel. Con 16 de 20 o más, pasa al siguiente.
  6. A las 12 semanas, repite el diagnóstico y compara tu resultado.

Lecturas con comprensión (A2, B1, B2)

Lee cada texto dos veces: la primera sin diccionario, para la idea general, y la segunda para contestar las preguntas.

Lectura A2 · A day in my life

My name is Daniel and I'm 20 years old. I study computer engineering at a university in Mexico. From Monday to Friday, I get up at six o'clock. I take a shower, have a quick breakfast and take the bus to school. The trip takes about forty minutes, so I usually listen to music or podcasts in English.

My classes start at seven thirty and finish at two. My favorite subject is programming because I love solving problems. After class, I have lunch with my friends in the cafeteria. In the afternoon, I work part-time at a computer repair shop. I fix laptops and install software.

I get home at eight. I do my homework, chat with my girlfriend and go to bed at eleven. On weekends, I sleep late, play soccer and visit my grandparents.

Preguntas

  1. How does Daniel go to school?
  2. What does he do on the bus?
  3. Why does he like programming?
  4. Where does he work?
  5. True or false: Daniel's classes finish at seven thirty.
  6. True or false: He visits his grandparents on weekends.
  7. Find a word in the text that means «medio tiempo».

Lectura B1 · How I learned to speak English

When I started university, my English was terrible. I could read a little, but I couldn't understand people when they spoke, and I was too shy to say anything. One day, a professor told us that most of the documentation we would use in our careers was in English. That was when I decided to change.

First, I made a simple plan. Every morning, I listened to a short podcast for learners on my way to class. At first I understood very little, but after a few weeks I noticed that I could follow the main ideas. Then I started writing three or four sentences every night about my day. I used an online tool to check my mistakes and kept a list of the errors I made most often.

Speaking was the hardest part. I joined an online conversation group that met twice a week. The first sessions were stressful, and I felt embarrassed when I made mistakes. However, the other members were friendly, and I realized that everyone was making mistakes too.

After a year, I took a free online test and got a B1 level. I'm not fluent yet, but I can have a conversation, watch videos about technology without subtitles and read documentation without translating every word. If I had known how much small daily habits help, I would have started much earlier.

Preguntas

  1. What were the writer's two main problems at the beginning?
  2. What made the writer decide to improve?
  3. Name two daily habits the writer started.
  4. Why were the first conversation sessions stressful?
  5. True or false: The writer is fluent now.
  6. True or false: The writer took a free online test after one year.
  7. What does embarrassed mean in the text?
  8. Copy the third conditional sentence from the text.

Lectura B2 · Will AI replace programmers?

Every few years, a new technology arrives with the promise, or the threat, of making programmers unnecessary. Visual programming tools, website builders and low-code platforms were all expected to reduce the need for developers. Today, the same question is being asked about artificial intelligence.

There is no doubt that AI tools have changed how software is written. Many developers now use assistants that suggest code, explain error messages and generate tests. Tasks that used to take an afternoon can sometimes be completed in minutes. As a result, some companies are rethinking how many junior developers they need.

However, writing code has never been the whole job. Developers spend a large part of their time understanding problems, talking to clients, making design decisions and fixing systems that fail in unexpected ways. An AI assistant can produce a function quickly, but someone still has to decide whether that function should exist, check that it is secure and take responsibility when something goes wrong.

What is likely to change is the kind of skills that are valued. Programmers who can communicate clearly, review code critically and understand the business behind the software will probably be in a stronger position than those who only know one programming language. Learning never stops in this field: the tools that are popular today may be obsolete in five years.

In other words, AI may not replace programmers, but programmers who know how to work with AI might replace those who don't. For students, the best strategy is to learn the fundamentals well, practice with modern tools and keep improving their English, the language in which most of these tools are documented.

Preguntas

  1. What is the main idea of the text? (a) AI will soon replace all programmers. (b) AI is changing programming, but the job involves much more than writing code. (c) Low-code platforms have failed.
  2. According to the text, what can AI assistants do? Name three things.
  3. Why does the writer say that writing code «has never been the whole job»?
  4. Which skills will probably be more valued in the future?
  5. Explain in your own words: programmers who know how to work with AI might replace those who don't.
  6. Find two examples of the passive voice.
  7. What does obsolete mean?
  8. Do you agree with the writer? Write 3 or 4 sentences.

Lista de control

  • Cada oración tiene sujeto y verbo.
  • Revisé los tiempos verbales: did + forma base y -s con he / she / it.
  • Usé al menos 3 conectores distintos.
  • No traduje palabra por palabra (revisa los falsos amigos del documento 05).
  • Revisé las mayúsculas: I, días, meses, idiomas y nacionalidades.
  • Leí el texto en voz alta antes de darlo por terminado.

Práctica de speaking

Habla en voz alta 10 minutos al día: responde una pregunta, grábate un minuto y evalúate con la rúbrica.

Método del minuto

  1. Elige una pregunta de tu nivel.
  2. Piensa 30 segundos y anota solo 3 palabras clave.
  3. Habla 1 minuto sin parar y grábate.
  4. Escúchate y anota 2 errores, 1 palabra que te faltó y 1 cosa que hiciste bien.
  5. Repite la misma pregunta al día siguiente y compara.

Práctica de listening guiada

Cada semana completa una de estas 4 rutinas con audios gratuitos oficiales; llevan de 15 a 40 minutos.

Rutina 1 · BBC 6 Minute English (B1–B2, 20 minutos)

  1. Abre un episodio en 6 Minute English y lee solo el título. Escribe 3 palabras que creas que vas a oír.
  2. Escucha sin transcripción y escribe la idea principal en una oración.
  3. Escucha otra vez y anota 5 palabras nuevas.
  4. Lee la transcripción mientras escuchas y subraya las frases útiles.
  5. Haz el quiz o la hoja de trabajo del episodio.
  6. Usa 2 palabras nuevas en oraciones propias.

Rutina 2 · ELLLO (A1–C1, 15 minutos)

  1. Elige en ELLLO una lección de tu nivel.
  2. Escucha una vez y responde el quiz.
  3. Revisa la transcripción y repite 3 frases en voz alta imitando al hablante (shadowing).

Rutina 3 · Dictado con VOA Learning English (A2–B1, 15 minutos)

  1. Escoge en VOA Learning English una noticia corta con audio.
  2. Escucha frase por frase y escribe todo lo que oyes.
  3. Compara con el texto y marca lo que no oíste. Si fueron palabras pequeñas (a, the, of), es normal: en inglés suenan muy débiles.

Rutina 4 · Examen de muestra de Cambridge (A2–B2, 40 minutos, una vez al mes)

  1. Descarga el examen de muestra de tu nivel: A2 Key, B1 Preliminary o B2 First.
  2. Haz la sección de listening sin pausas, como en el examen real.
  3. Califícate con la hoja de respuestas y lee la transcripción de las preguntas que fallaste.
